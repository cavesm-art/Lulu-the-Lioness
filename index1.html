Before you begin

Over the next couple of days you will investigate why lions live in groups, or prides. You will work through four data sets, discuss your findings with your team, and answer the driving questions in a Final Report.

In this version you do more than read. You will move through the field notes day by day, sort chromosomes into a karyotype, run a hunting experiment, and test possible parents against the DNA. In most places you make a prediction or calculation first, then check it.

Your voice and opinions matter. The loudest person isn't always the one with the best answers. Talk with your team, then write your own answers.

Glossary
Pride
A social group of lions: related females, their cubs, and a few adult males.
Patriarch
An adult male that holds the pride and defends it.
Chromosome
A long strand of DNA that carries many genes. Lions have 38.
Karyotype
An organized picture of an individual's chromosomes, arranged in matching pairs.
Gene and locus
A gene is a section of DNA that affects a trait. A locus (plural: loci) is the spot on a chromosome where a gene sits. Here the loci are FCA26, FCA45, FCA77, and FCA96.
Allele
One version of a gene. Here they are written as letters.
Genotype
The two alleles an individual has at a locus. You get one allele from each parent.
Parentage
Who an individual's biological parents are.
